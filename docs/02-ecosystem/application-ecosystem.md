# Royal City — Application Ecosystem

Royal City is one ecosystem, not one undifferentiated application.

## Application principle

Applications are experience and operational surfaces over shared Royal City identity, authorization, actions, services, state, events and records.

They do not create parallel authority systems.

## Canonical application families

### 1. Personal applications

For a person to manage:

- identity and account;
- participations;
- places and access;
- services;
- requests;
- payments;
- activity;
- notifications;
- authorized data use;
- personal settings.

### 2. Community operating applications

For participating community management environments to coordinate:

- people/participation;
- properties and places;
- facilities;
- utilities;
- resources;
- service providers;
- operations;
- community economic activity;
- community policies;
- incidents and records.

The community application operates through community onboarding credentials and community authority. It does not become a person-style Royal City account.

### 3. Provider operating applications

For independent providers to manage:

- provider participation;
- workers;
- services;
- service offerings;
- availability;
- assignments;
- requests;
- execution;
- provider-owned records;
- community relationships.

Provider authority remains bounded by provider ownership/governance and Royal City authorization where the action is Royal City-governed.

### 4. Royal City operating applications

For Royal City to operate the NOS and Royal City-owned services:

- identity/network integrity;
- authorization;
- platform operations;
- ecosystem coordination;
- Royal City-owned services;
- security;
- governance;
- integration management;
- observability.

### 5. Service applications

Service-specific experiences may exist for:

- access;
- home/property;
- maintenance;
- facilities;
- utilities;
- payments;
- mobility;
- booking;
- commerce;
- delivery;
- education;
- health;
- work;
- communication;
- future services.

A service application does not become a separate identity or authorization authority.

### 6. Operational workspaces

Royal City may provide:

- personal workspace;
- community operating workspace;
- provider operating workspace;
- Royal City operating workspace;
- team workspace;
- project workspace;
- incident workspace;
- review workspace.

Workspace visibility and membership never become authority.

## Shared platform substrate

All applications use the same conceptual substrate:

`Identity → Relationship → Authority → Authorization → Action → State → Event → Evidence`

This is the principal mechanism by which many applications remain one Royal City ecosystem.

## Application boundary rule

An application may own its user experience and operational workflows, but canonical identity, authorization, consequential action semantics, and shared event/evidence rules remain governed by Royal City platform contracts.

## External application participation

External community/provider applications may participate through explicit contracts and adapters without becoming Royal City-owned applications.

Royal City should distinguish:

- application ownership;
- operational ownership;
- data ownership;
- source of truth;
- integration participation;
- authorization responsibility;
- execution responsibility.
