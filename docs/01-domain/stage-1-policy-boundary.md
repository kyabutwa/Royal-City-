# Royal City — Stage 1 Policy Boundary

## Why this exists

A domain model can establish structural truths without inventing policy.

Royal City already establishes structural principles such as:

- unified identity
- separate authorization
- contextual authority
- first-class relationships
- action-centered interaction
- voluntary participation
- distinction between internal and external authority

The following are policy choices that cannot be derived safely from those principles alone.

## Policy decisions

### Identity policy
Who may create, verify, suspend, revoke, or recover identities?

### Authority policy
Which actors possess authority, and which authority wins when legitimate authorities conflict?

### Relationship policy
Which relationships exist and what powers do they create?

### Community policy
How do communities form, govern, change rules, and resolve conflicts?

### Physical-world policy
Who owns, controls, operates, and accesses buildings, units, facilities, and infrastructure?

### Service policy
What obligations exist between providers and consumers?

### Economic policy
What economic activities are internal, external, reversible, disputable, or final?

### Record policy
What must be recorded, who controls records, and what correction/retention rights exist?

### Emergency policy
Whether extraordinary authority exists and under what safeguards.

### Boundary policy
Where Royal City's authority ends and another system's authority begins.

## Rule

These decisions require Royal City product/domain ownership.

The repository must not silently resolve them.
